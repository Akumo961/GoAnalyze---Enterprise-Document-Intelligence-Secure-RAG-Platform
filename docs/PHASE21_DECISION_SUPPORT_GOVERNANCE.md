# Phase 21 — Decision-Support Governance

## Purpose

GoAnalyze Government is **decision-support software**. It assists authorized analysts by organizing evidence, retrieving source material, highlighting potential gaps, and presenting explainable model output. It must not autonomously issue, approve, deny, vary, suspend, or otherwise make a legally binding environmental decision.

## Human accountability

A legally consequential determination remains the responsibility of an authorized human decision-maker under the customer's governance and delegated-authority framework.

The platform therefore distinguishes:

- **Factual evidence** — information directly attributable to an ingested source.
- **Inference** — an analytical conclusion derived from available evidence.
- **Model-generated summary** — language generated by an AI model.
- **Uncertainty** — information that is missing, ambiguous, conflicting, low-confidence, or otherwise requires review.
- **Human decision** — an action explicitly recorded by an authorized analyst or decision-maker.

AI output must not be represented as a human decision.

## Required workflow boundary

The supported workflow is:

1. AI/document services collect and organize evidence.
2. Retrieval returns source-grounded material and citations.
3. Analytical services may identify gaps, inconsistencies, or review priority.
4. The system routes the case for human review where configured.
5. An authorized human reviews the evidence and model output.
6. The human records the decision or disposition.
7. The audit trail records the human action and relevant evidence references.

There is no supported product workflow in which an LLM directly commits a legally binding environmental decision.

## AI output controls

Customer-facing implementations should enforce these controls at the service boundary:

- source citations for factual claims where source evidence is available;
- explicit labeling of model-generated content;
- explicit uncertainty when evidence is insufficient or conflicting;
- no fabricated regulatory requirements or citations;
- no silent elevation of model inference into authoritative fact;
- no direct LLM authority to change case disposition or legal status;
- authorization checks before retrieving protected evidence;
- tenant isolation before retrieval and response generation;
- human confirmation for consequential workflow transitions.

## Regulatory knowledge boundary

Regulatory mappings are only authoritative when loaded from customer-approved, governed sources. Demo or sample regulatory content must remain explicitly non-authoritative. The model must not invent a regulatory obligation when an authoritative source is unavailable.

## Audit requirements

For consequential workflows, implementations should retain enough evidence to reconstruct:

- who initiated the analysis;
- which documents and knowledge versions were available;
- which sources were cited;
- which model-generated output was presented;
- what uncertainty or warnings were displayed;
- who performed the human review;
- what human action was taken;
- when the action occurred;
- which authorization context permitted the action.

## Prohibited product claims

The repository must not claim that GoAnalyze:

- makes autonomous legal decisions;
- replaces government decision-makers;
- guarantees regulatory compliance;
- guarantees correctness of AI conclusions;
- provides authoritative regulatory interpretation without governed source data;
- has received government approval merely because this governance design exists.

## Verification status

This document defines the product governance boundary. It is not evidence of legal compliance, certification, government approval, or an external AI-risk assessment. Those require customer-specific policy, legal/privacy review, security assessment, and other appropriate external validation.
